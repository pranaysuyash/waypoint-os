    what it catches

    What’s missing matters more than what’s there.

    Any tool can lift the destination out of a message. The expensive mistakes are the blanks — the room type nobody mentioned, the passport that expires mid-trip, the budget nobody wants to say out loud. Waypoint separates them by consequence, so you know what to chase and what to stop worrying about.

    Blocks the trip

    Missing these means the itinerary cannot be booked or is actively risky.

    • passport validity vs. travel dates
    • visa lead time for the destination
    • minimum connection windows
    • traveller names as printed

    Changes the quote

    The number you send moves materially depending on these.

    • budget ceiling and currency
    • room category and bed setup
    • arrival and departure city
    • internal transport class

    Shapes the plan

    Nice to know. Worth asking, never worth blocking a quote on.

    • pace preference
    • dietary requirements
    • accessibility needs
    • celebration moments
    how it works

    Four steps, one brief, no retyping.

    The whole point is that the request never has to be read twice. It arrives unstructured and leaves as something you can price.

    01

    The request lands however it lands

    Forward the thread, paste the notes, or drop in the transcript. There is no form for the client to fill in and no new tool for your team to learn first.

    02

    Fields come out labelled by confidence

    Every value lands as fact, inferred, assumed, or unknown. You can see which lines the system is sure about and which ones it guessed — before that guess reaches a client.

    03

    Gaps get ranked, then asked

    Waypoint hands you the questions worth sending, ordered by how much they change the quote. Copy the reply draft and send it, or edit it first.

    04

    The brief goes to quote with the record attached

    What came from the client, what the system assumed, and what is still open travels with the brief. When someone asks why a trip was priced that way, the answer is already written down.

    for agencies

    Built for small teams doing careful work.

    Waypoint assumes a handful of advisors, a queue of enquiries, and an owner who reviews anything risky. If that is not your shop, this is probably not your tool — and that is a fair thing to know before you sign up.

    A good fit if

    • Enquiries arrive as free text across several channels.
    • Someone re-reads the same thread to build the same brief.
    • Quotes have gone out with a detail nobody confirmed.
    • You want the reasoning written down, not just the output.

    Not a good fit if

    • You need a client-facing booking portal out of the box.
    • Your enquiries already arrive in a fixed structured form.
    • You want fully automated quoting with no human review.
    start where you already are

    Bring your messiest thread. See what comes out.

    Paste a real enquiry on the first run. If Waypoint cannot pull a usable brief out of it, you will know in about ninety seconds and it will not have cost you a rollout.
