The actual problem

The work isn't planning the trip. It's finding out what you don't know yet.

A request arrives as three messages and a forwarded PDF. Somewhere in there is a destination, a window, a budget you had to guess at, and a dietary requirement nobody wrote down. That gap is where quotes go wrong — and it's invisible until a traveller asks why the invoice changed.

Generic AI fills the gap

Ask a chatbot to summarise that thread and you get a confident paragraph. It reads well and it is wrong in the places that cost you money — because the model cannot tell the difference between what was said and what it made up.

Waypoint marks the gap

Every field carries its own status: fact, inferred, assumed, or unknown. Unknowns stay empty and generate the question you'd have asked anyway. You can see the seams before you commit to a number.

How it works

Four steps, and you can stop at any of them.

  1. 01

    Forward what you already have

    WhatsApp exports, email threads, call notes, a PDF from another agency. No new forms, no structured intake to fill in first.

  2. 02

    Read the brief it builds

    Every extracted field is labelled by how it was arrived at. Open any field to see the source line it came from and what happens if it's wrong.

  3. 03

    Answer only what's actually missing

    Unknowns become drafted questions you can send as-is. You're not re-checking forty fields — you're closing three.

  4. 04

    Work the trip from there

    Proposal, supplier chases, and client updates carry the same provenance forward, so nothing silently becomes a fact on the way out.

Fit

  • Requests arrive as messages, not forms — and that isn't going to change.

Not a good fit if
