# Practitioner review packet for itinerary feasibility

24 November 2026 · Recruitment and independent review material

## Participant brief

You are reviewing short fictional trip proposals and disruptions. For each assigned case, imagine you are responsible for the next customer or supplier interaction. Record what you would verify, what you would say to the traveler, and whether you could proceed. Names of places and operators are real where relevant. Times, rates and booking details are test inputs. For any actual rule you rely on, give the source, the applicable product and date, and any limit on what it establishes. Please avoid including real customer records, booking references, documents or unpublished supplier terms in your answers.

Review the base case first. Then read the two changes separately, each against the original, and state what changes in your decision. The altered facts help show whether a check can recognize an exception or should ask for more information. Allow 25 minutes for four cases. Use any relevant source you normally consult.

### Supplemental transport case F06-R

Two independently purchased legs are Eurostar and TGV INOUI via Paris. The first train is delayed, so the traveler expects to miss the onward train. Ask the reviewer for the first useful instruction at the station and what evidence is needed before promising continuation. Then substitute a RER or a separately booked driver for the onward leg. For the uncertain case, keep the operator or onward station missing. Record whether a waiting period, seat availability, or lack of a substitute would change the advice.

**Evidence and expected scope.** Eurostar distinguishes a through contract from conditional journey continuation on eligible separately ticketed trains. Its operator guidance lists route and operator restrictions, availability and proof of disruption. The separate driver is a different supplier commitment. A RER leg is excluded from the published HOTNAT conditions. A missing operator or station warrants a question, and a possible replacement seat is separate from the feasibility of the original transfer. British National Rail has its own ticket and connection rules, so do not extend either operator’s conditions to the other. [Eurostar connecting trains](https://www.eurostar.com/uk-en/travel-info/connecting-train) · [Eurostar missed-connection conditions](https://help.eurostar.com/faq/uk-en/question/What-happens-if-I-miss-my-connection-due-to-a-delay-or-cancellation) · [National Rail changing trains](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/travel-information/changing-trains/)

**Waypoint ownership.** Record contractual linkage, any operator continuation program, station and transfer margin, and the disruption evidence as separate facts in the existing feasibility workflow. The traveler gets the first question or operator action. Workbench can retain the applicable source and whether an advisor confirmed it for the actual booking. On 25 November all four standalone disruption variants were replayed locally on both endpoints. Each omitted a scoped feasibility question. [Observed coverage and source evidence](ITINERARY_CHECKS_INDUSTRY_COUNCIL_2026-11-24.md#when-one-itinerary-contains-several-commitments)

**Optional combined case for independent review.** Give the practitioner this message before showing the checker result. “The overnight train is waitlisted. Our morning transfer is booked separately. One guest needs step-free boarding. The van and station transfer are unconfirmed. The hotel needs an advance today. At what point do I lose that money if I cancel?” Ask which action they would take first, which issues they would keep open and who can resolve each one. If the hotel confirms the rate deadline, ask what they still need for the transfer. Record the reasoning when the actual pickup deadline or party size is unknown.
