## Verdict

**Test a pre-booking second opinion that helps a traveler make one decision.** Use “Check your itinerary before you book” as the public headline and show a single labeled sample finding beside paste/upload. A traveler should be able to say what to do, why, and what still needs confirmation. Lead with the report. Put the map, simulations and conditional hotel benefits behind findings they clarify.

The marketing promise overlaps with established tools. Treat differentiation as a hypothesis about evidence quality, specificity and follow-through. Start with multi-leg DIY or AI-assisted plans where one transfer or booking assumption is consequential. Validate a named, staffed agency pilot separately. Public acquisition, agency retention and willingness to pay are three hypotheses with different buyers and evidence.

## Proposed screen hierarchy

| State | Content order and interaction |
|---|---|
| Public entry | “Check your itinerary before you book.” One sentence on transfer, pacing and details you may need to confirm. Paste field is visible before scrolling on mobile where practical. Secondary upload option, “See a sample report,” and concise data-use copy beside input. |
| Agency entry | Named agency and advisor where confirmed, identical input mechanics, clear explanation of who runs the check and when the advisor can see the plan. Unknown link and temporarily unavailable agency get distinct recovery paths. |
| Parsing and clarification | Preserve original text. Report progress without fabricated steps. Show parsed facts with edit controls and one decision-changing question at a time. Allow continuation with explicit uncertainty where useful. |
| Decision report | Display top action first, then findings ordered by consequence, what was checked, and what remains uncertain. Each finding includes the segment, evidence, assumption or estimate, and action. Show source and date where available. |
| No issues or unavailable evidence | “No issues found in the checks we could run” with coverage. For unavailable data, show the gap and a manual verification action. For a failed run, retain input and allow retry. |
| Evidence and visuals | Add a time-budget diagram only when grounded in a specific transfer. Render an equivalent text summary. Label simulation controls and assumptions next to their values. Maps support spatial comparisons when real legs exist. |
| Share and advisor exit | Public report offers a redacted summary and a notification opt-in when supply is absent. Agency report names the receiving team and previews fields before consent. Show failed delivery and retry when supported. |

**Sample finding copy, with fictional inputs:** “Confirm whether your flights are on one ticket.” “Your plan shows 50 minutes between flights. We don’t know whether bags are checked through. Ask the airline before buying. If these are separate tickets, compare a later departure.” The number belongs to the labeled sample. A live finding should show where its time came from and flag missing terminal or ticket details.

**Visual direction:** Use a readable report layout with deliberate editorial hierarchy, restrained accent, and a single strong primary action. Reuse the existing typography and spacing system first. Use tabular numerals for times and amounts. Use severity words alongside color, readable status text, and a compact evidence disclosure. On wide layouts the input and labeled sample can sit side by side. Stack input first on mobile and avoid horizontal navigation for core findings. Optional visuals should earn their space by explaining a decision. Preserve layout stability during loading and respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Implementation handoff and pilot evidence

1. **Trust contract.** Define allowed evidence states (user supplied, source verified, estimated, unknown, sample). Implement report-first hierarchy and dedicated sample rendering. Owner lane: checker frontend plus diagnostic schema. Acceptance: supported and unsupported fixtures, including a live-style packet with zero trustworthy figures, never show sample values as traveler data.
2. **Accessible entry and report.** Apply form semantics, concise errors, status announcements, and an accessible visualization disclosure. Owner lane: frontend. Acceptance: input-to-result at 320 and 390 CSS px and desktop, keyboard and screen reader checks, 200% zoom, rendered contrast checks, reduced motion, and measured layout stability. Capture before/after on the same viewport.
3. **Truthful branded handoff.** Resolve profile state, recipient, consent, delivery and queue acknowledgement. Verify ownership and report lifecycle with isolated fixtures. Owner lanes: backend and agency operations. Acceptance: named recipient receives the expected redacted brief, traveler sees a faithful receipt, unknown/unavailable agency and failed delivery remain recoverable. Exercise duplicate submission and retry.
4. **Measurement and pilot.** Instrument intake start, valid input, actionable report, evidence opened, action understood, consent granted, brief delivered, accepted and contacted. Keep synthetic/demo traffic out of acquisition and fulfillment denominators. Record minimal event metadata. Owner lanes: growth and a named agency operator. Test current positioning versus the second-opinion proposition with identical sample evidence, then test whether the report changes a booking decision.

**Proposed pilot bar:** Begin with 5–8 representative travelers and one staffed advisor team. Use realistic sanitized notes, including transfer ambiguity, wrong property, unsupported data, and a clean result. In moderated sessions ask travelers to state action and uncertainty unaided, then record whether they independently verify or change a choice. A proposed bar is 4 of the first 5 explaining both correctly within one minute of viewing the result, with zero misleading source claims, surprise data transfers or false delivery confirmations. This is a formative decision rule. Measure time to first actionable finding, valid runs per valid input, and each fulfillment stage independently. Test willingness to pay with a concrete agency offer after the pilot shows trip briefs are useful. Revisit the wedge if evidence adds little beyond generic advice, or if advisors cannot economically fulfill the handoff.

**Open owner decisions:** Which booking decision and corridor to pilot first, which agency will staff the pilot, and how response expectations are set. Wider matching and persistent sharing require separate fulfillment and access decisions. Workbench time savings and proposed pricing remain business hypotheses.
