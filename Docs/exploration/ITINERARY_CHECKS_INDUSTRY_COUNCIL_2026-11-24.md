# Itinerary check coverage and Waypoint OS ownership

November 24, 2026 · Research and architecture review · [GTM strategy](../GTM_MULTI_STREAM_WEDGE_SYNTHESIS_2026-11-07.md) · [Goa replay and prior evidence](../worklogs/2026-11-24-checker-finding-recheck-and-learning.md)

## Decision

**Itinerary feasibility is already part of the Waypoint OS core.** This review concerns specific checks within that core, starting with day-level pacing and overnight hotel timing. A traveler can use the public checker without involving an advisor. In the B2B product, the same evidence should help an advisor review an incoming plan, check their own draft before sending it, and revisit a booked trip after a change. The advisor view adds source detail, verification ownership, supplier correspondence and resolution history. The traveler view exposes the finding, what remains uncertain, and a concrete next step appropriate to the stage.

The review used three independent simulated industry seats: agency operations, destination and supplier operations, and senior advisor/traveler communication. They inspected relevant source and worked through different kinds of itineraries. Public research used carrier, operator, government and standards sources. This is not practitioner research, production coverage evidence, or a claim that any cited trip can be booked. Specific schedules, access policies, data rights and coverage require confirmation before use.

## Council working notes

- **Agency operations.** The same audit must be useful on an advisor's draft and on an incoming client plan. The core needs the factual promise, the affected booking, the supplier to contact and the deadline. Reuse connection and accessibility code after removing inputs that masquerade as confirmed facts. The proposed check should reduce repeated questions and work on a real agency's existing intake workflow.
- **Destination and supplier operations.** Findings should distinguish scheduled arrival, actual arrival at a gate or property, hard cutoff, recommended buffer, and contractual protection. Similar-looking bookings can have different rules. Capture the exact operator, product, itinerary date and source. Use scoped advice when evidence stops short of a verdict.
- **Frontline advisor and traveler experience.** Put the affected line of the submitted itinerary beside the finding. Explain what it means and whom to ask. For an unknown, expose the first missing fact and make it editable. The advisor should see the same finding and provenance as the traveler, together with evidence and resolution history. Keep share/export and receiving brief consistent.

All three seats were simulated. **No external operations planner, ticketing specialist, or travel advisor was contacted.** Public operator and government documentation is industry evidence about published terms, not feedback from a practitioner on the product. The practice review remains an open step with named roles and case prompts below.

## One check, two surfaces

Use the shared fact and risk models. A reusable result needs a stable check/version, itinerary version and affected segment, the facts and source spans it used, applicability and time basis, source link and freshness, outcome, and one next action. Track severity independently of certainty. Treat missing input, out of coverage, provider unavailable, checked-clear and concern as distinct outcomes.

Supply scoped rules from a maintained source catalog. A rule can express an applicable operator deadline, expected access steps, or conditions for abstention. Preserve supplier and agency overrides with source, reviewer, and audit trail. Deterministic code owns time arithmetic, outputs, currentness, eligibility and blockers. Use an evaluated model, if useful, for extracting roles, dates, segments and evidence spans from messy notes. Its output goes through the same validation as the deterministic extractor. Reserve advisory-only behavior for checks whose truth depends on an unknown or unverified source.

The advisor surface should show the itinerary version, evidence and remaining question, the party responsible for confirmation, and the deadline if known. The traveler can see the same concern with plain language and a scoped next action. On repeat, identify which finding changed and why. Keep report, export and Workbench references consistent. Treat request capture, queue write, acknowledgment and resolution as distinct states.
